# Brand, Marketing, and Story

## Purpose

Create distinctive, truthful brand and marketing systems that move an audience from present understanding to useful action without manipulation or generic template language.

This skill synthesizes original operating rules inspired by established usability, brand, storytelling, and influence literature. It is not a substitute for the source books and must not reproduce copyrighted passages.

## Brand operating sequence

`AUDIENCE -> PROBLEM -> POSITION -> PROMISE -> PROOF -> PERSONALITY -> SYSTEM -> EXPERIENCE -> MEASURE`

### Audience

Describe the actual audience's context, language, anxieties, motivations, alternatives, and decision environment. Avoid demographic stereotypes.

### Problem

Name the meaningful job, tension, or unmet need. Do not manufacture pain.

### Position

Answer:

- What category are we in?
- What do we do differently?
- Why should the intended audience care?
- What would we never want to be confused with?

If the position could describe ten competitors, it is not finished.

### Promise

One clear, supportable value proposition. No unsupported superlatives.

### Proof

Prioritize:

- outcomes;
- artifacts;
- demonstrations;
- customer/community evidence;
- process transparency;
- credible third-party proof.

### Personality

Define behavioral voice, not adjective soup. Example dimensions: direct vs ceremonial, playful vs restrained, technical vs plainspoken.

### System

Turn the identity into reusable rules for typography, layout, imagery, color, motion, copy, story, and calls to action.

## Story workflow

For any campaign/story, identify:

1. audience's current story/belief;
2. attention/hook;
3. human tension or stakes;
4. credible journey or evidence;
5. shift in understanding;
6. possible future;
7. specific next action.

A story should earn attention, challenge or expand a useful belief, and leave the audience with a credible path forward.

Stories must be relevant, concise enough for the medium, emotionally honest, aligned with the strategic objective, and supported by evidence where factual claims are made.

## Marketing doctrine

Prefer relationship value over purely transactional extraction. Every campaign should answer:

- Why is this useful to the audience?
- Why now?
- Why us?
- What proof reduces uncertainty?
- What is the smallest clear next action?
- How will we learn from response?

Operate as:

`WRITE/BUILD -> SHIP SAFELY -> TEST -> MEASURE -> LEARN -> REFINE`

## Influence guardrail

Language frameworks may be used to reduce ambiguity, make options easier to understand, invite reflection, and ask for next steps.

Never use them to:

- conceal material facts;
- manufacture scarcity;
- impersonate consent;
- pressure vulnerable people;
- make a false outcome seem guaranteed;
- bypass a required approval;
- create deceptive social proof.

When persuasion and informed choice conflict, informed choice wins.

## Creative quality review

Check:

- distinctiveness;
- clarity;
- relevance;
- emotional truth;
- proof;
- memorability;
- simplicity;
- consistency;
- accessibility;
- appropriate surprise;
- whether beauty strengthens meaning rather than masking weakness.

## Deliverable rule

Do not output generic `brand strategy` prose. Produce the artifact the project needs: position statement, message hierarchy, campaign brief, story map, content system, offer page, creative direction, scripts, or review rubric.
