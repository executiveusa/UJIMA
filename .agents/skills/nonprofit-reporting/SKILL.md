# Nonprofit Reporting

## Governing skill

Inherit `.agents/skills/nonprofit-operating-system/SKILL.md`.

## Mission

Give the client one simple view of what happened, what mattered, what was learned, and what needs approval.

## Monthly report structure

### VISIBILITY
- posts published
- videos published
- views
- reach

### WEBSITE
- visitors
- traffic sources
- meaningful conversions

### COMMUNITY
- new family inquiries
- volunteer inquiries
- partner inquiries
- donors/supporters where relevant

### BEST CONTENT
- top post/video
- why it likely worked, clearly marked as analysis rather than fact when inferred

### WHAT WE LEARNED
- 2–3 useful insights

### NEXT MONTH
- what the team will do

### NEEDS APPROVAL
- only consequential decisions

## Proof rules

Separate VERIFIED FACT, CLIENT-SUPPLIED FACT, OBSERVED EVIDENCE, INFERENCE, RECOMMENDATION, and UNKNOWN.

Never manufacture complete-period totals from partial data. Never report reach, conversions, donations, inquiries, or outcomes that were not actually measured.

## Client experience

Do not make the dashboard the deliverable. Translate the data into outcomes and decisions.

Prefer:
“312 people found your organization this month. 27 took action.”

over:
“Here is Google Analytics.”

## Success test

Each reporting cycle asks whether the organization:
- reached more people;
- gained more trust;
- generated more useful actions;
- saved founder/staff time;
- improved digital presence;
- collected better data;
- followed up more reliably;
- created new opportunities.

If the answer is no, recommend what to change rather than hiding behind activity metrics.
