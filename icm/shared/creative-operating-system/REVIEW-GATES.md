# Mandatory Review Gates

No artifact advances because it looks polished or because a deadline is close. It advances only when the applicable gates pass.

## Gate 1 — Purpose

- One measurable objective is stated.
- One primary audience is stated.
- One primary action is stated.
- The artifact is necessary for the campaign.

Failure response: return to brief.

## Gate 2 — Human truth

- The work begins with a real need, person, or verified community context.
- Subjects are not reduced to labels or hardship.
- Story, quote, image, and emotion are not fabricated.

Failure response: stop production and repair source material.

## Gate 3 — Clarity and usability

- A first-time user understands the purpose in ten seconds.
- Essential facts are visible.
- There is one dominant action.
- Labels are literal and action-oriented.
- Mobile, accessibility, feedback, and error recovery are addressed.

Failure response: simplify and retest.

## Gate 4 — Story and retention

- Subject, desire, obstacle, stakes, and change are present where appropriate.
- The opening earns attention honestly.
- Each segment progresses.
- The payoff matches the promise.
- No bait-and-switch or manufactured urgency is present.

Failure response: restructure rather than decorate.

## Gate 5 — Evidence and accuracy

- Names, dates, locations, roles, affiliations, counts, and claims are verified.
- Targets, estimates, observations, and outcomes are distinguished.
- Source notes and provenance are present.
- Sponsor and partner status is confirmed.

Failure response: block release until verified or remove the claim.

## Gate 6 — Safety, consent, and dignity

- Consent is documented for identifiable people where required.
- Guardian approval exists for identifiable minors.
- Sensitive data has been minimized.
- Foreseeable harm has been evaluated.
- A named safeguarding approver has reviewed high-risk material.

Failure response: publication blocked. This gate cannot be waived by creative leadership.

## Gate 7 — Brand and craft

- The artifact aligns with tenant mission, voice, design tokens, and promise.
- Visual hierarchy is deliberate.
- Copy is concise.
- Decoration does not compete with meaning.
- The work is original and not an imitation of a reference organization or creator.

Failure response: revise or reject.

## Gate 8 — Production readiness

- Required dimensions, formats, captions, language versions, alt text, masters, filenames, deadlines, and backups are defined.
- Links, forms, QR codes, webhooks, and destinations have been tested.
- Approved, restricted, and raw assets are separated.

Failure response: do not schedule or publish.

## Gate 9 — Approval and release

Required lifecycle:

`DRAFT -> INTERNAL_REVIEW -> CLIENT_REVIEW -> APPROVED -> SCHEDULED -> EXECUTED -> VERIFIED -> ARCHIVED`

An approval record must include:

- artifact ID and version;
- approver identity and role;
- date and time;
- channels and dates authorized;
- evidence bundle;
- restrictions;
- rollback or cancellation method.

Silence, prior approval of another version, or informal chat approval is insufficient for sensitive content.

## Gate 10 — Outcome and learning

After release:

- verify execution;
- record outcomes and errors;
- preserve evidence;
- identify reusable assets;
- log lessons;
- propose source-level fixes for recurring defects.

## GRILL overlay

Before final approval, answer:

- **Gaps:** What is missing or assumed?
- **Risks:** What could harm people, trust, delivery, or the organization?
- **Impact:** What real outcome should change?
- **Leverage:** What can be reused across channels or future campaigns?
- **Legacy:** Will the organization be proud of this record in five years?

An unresolved high-risk GRILL finding blocks release.
