## Law 1 — Dignity is non-negotiable

Never depict a person as broken, helpless, disposable, dangerous, or valuable only because their hardship can attract attention. Describe barriers without defining people by them.

## Law 2 — Consent is informed, specific, and reversible

Consent must identify what will be captured, how it may be used, where it may appear, and who can authorize use. Written guardian consent is required for identifiable minors. A release does not eliminate the duty to avoid harm.

## Law 3 — Minimize sensitive data

Collect only what the program or campaign actually requires. Do not place youth records, family situations, health information, school details, addresses, or private communications in model context or public repositories unless explicitly approved and protected.

## Law 4 — Claims require evidence

Program, funding, partnership, attendance, safety, and impact claims must be supported by records. Label targets as targets, estimates as estimates, and testimonials as individual experience.

## Law 5 — Transparency must be useful

Transparency is not a data dump. Report what was promised, what occurred, what resources were used, what remains uncertain, and what will happen next. Use plain language and provide supporting records where appropriate.

## Law 6 — Mission must match behavior

Do not publish values the organization is unwilling or unable to practice. When reality falls short, acknowledge the gap and define corrective action rather than covering it with branding.

## Law 7 — Community members are participants, not raw material

Include the people served in discovery, interpretation, and review. Do not extract stories without giving subjects context, respect, and a reasonable voice in how sensitive material is represented.

## Law 8 — Safeguarding outranks content opportunity

Stop capture or publication when safety, privacy, coercion, family conflict, legal exposure, or emotional distress is present. No shot, quote, campaign deadline, or sponsor request overrides safeguarding.

## Law 9 — Separate fundraising from pressure

A donation invitation must be truthful, voluntary, clear about use, and free from invented urgency, shame, or exploitation. Never imply that a specific child will lose support because a viewer does not donate unless that claim is literally true and authorized.

## Law 10 — Public correction is part of trust

Material errors must be corrected promptly. Preserve an audit note describing what changed, why, who approved the correction, and whether affected partners or subjects were notified.

## Law 11 — Approval follows risk

Mandatory human review applies to:

- identifiable minors;
- family or personal hardship;
- safety incidents;
- health, legal, financial, or disciplinary matters;
- donation-use claims;
- public partner announcements;
- political or faith-sensitive statements;
- outbound communication to youth;
- media interviews or crisis responses.

## Law 12 — Evidence belongs to the organization

Store releases, attendance records, partner confirmations, source notes, approved assets, outcome counts, and correction logs in exportable formats under organizational control.

## Approval checklist

- Does the artifact preserve dignity?
- Is consent specific and documented?
- Is any sensitive data unnecessary?
- Is every material claim supported?
- Are targets, estimates, and outcomes clearly distinguished?
- Does the public promise match actual operations?
- Were affected people included appropriately?
- Could publication create foreseeable harm?
- Is fundraising voluntary and transparent?
- Is there a named human approver and audit record?
