## Law 1 — The human is the entry point

Begin with a specific person, decision, desire, relationship, place, or moment. Statistics provide scale after the audience understands what is at stake.

Never reduce a person to a diagnosis, hardship, demographic label, or fundraising device.

## Law 2 — Every story needs change

A complete story establishes:

1. who the subject is;
2. what they want;
3. what stands in the way;
4. why it matters now;
5. what action or choice occurs;
6. what changes, remains unresolved, or becomes possible.

A chronology of activities is not automatically a story.

## Law 3 — Observe before directing

Documentary crews may organize logistics, lighting, and interview conditions, but must not manufacture emotion, ask people to repeat private moments, or stage false interactions.

When reconstruction is necessary, label it and obtain approval.

## Law 4 — Ask for meaning, not praise

Interview questions should surface memory, stakes, choices, and transformation.

Prefer:

- What made you decide to come?
- What did you notice today?
- What changed for you?
- What do you hope happens next?
- What would you tell someone who was not here?

Avoid questions that force endorsement or exaggeration.

## Law 5 — Context protects truth

Capture enough context to prevent a quote, image, or scene from creating a false impression. Record names, roles, dates, locations, consent status, and source notes at capture time.

## Law 6 — Every shot has a job

A shot must contribute at least one of:

- orientation;
- character;
- action;
- emotion;
- evidence;
- scale;
- transition;
- atmosphere;
- resolution.

Random coverage is not a production plan.

## Law 7 — Restraint preserves dignity

Do not use suffering, tears, private disclosures, dramatic music, slow motion, or close-ups simply to intensify emotion. Use the least manipulative treatment capable of communicating the truth.

## Law 8 — Accuracy outranks pace

Verify names, roles, claims, statistics, affiliations, dates, partner status, and outcomes. Distinguish observed fact, participant testimony, estimate, and organizational interpretation.

Correct material errors visibly and quickly.

## Law 9 — Plan delivery before capture

Before production, define:

- aspect ratios and crops;
- audio requirements;
- caption and subtitle requirements;
- language versions;
- file naming;
- release and consent metadata;
- master and derivative formats;
- backup locations;
- delivery deadlines;
- sponsor deliverables.

Do not discover technical requirements after the event.

## Law 10 — Capture the full arc

For events, record:

- empty location and setup;
- preparation and anticipation;
- arrivals and orientation;
- service or program action;
- details and relationships;
- challenges and adjustments;
- measurable proof;
- closing and cleanup;
- reflection after the event.

## Law 11 — Preserve subject voice

Edit for clarity without changing intent. Material changes to testimony require subject or authorized reviewer approval. Never combine separate statements to create a claim the speaker did not make.

## Law 12 — Accessibility is part of production

Every publishable video must have accurate captions. Important visual information should be understandable through audio description or accompanying text when needed. Language versions must preserve meaning rather than translate word-for-word without context.

## Approval checklist

- Does the story have a human subject, stakes, and change?
- Is every public claim sourced or marked for verification?
- Is consent documented for every identifiable minor?
- Could editing create a misleading impression?
- Does every selected shot contribute to the story or evidence?
- Are captions, language versions, and delivery formats planned?
- Are raw, approved, restricted, and published assets separated?
- Is dignity preserved even when the subject is vulnerable?
- Is sponsor recognition accurate and proportionate?
- Can the final artifact be traced back to source files and approvals?
