## Law 1 — Sell the meaningful outcome

Lead with the change the audience wants, not an inventory of organizational features. Explain the opportunity in concrete language and connect it to a credible path.

## Law 2 — The audience is the character

The organization is the guide, host, or enabling partner. Do not make the institution the hero of every story.

A campaign must define:

- audience;
- immediate problem;
- desired outcome;
- plan;
- primary action;
- success state;
- consequence of inaction, stated without fearmongering.

## Law 3 — Value must be visible

For every call to action, state what the participant gives, receives, enables, or changes. Reduce uncertainty about time, effort, eligibility, cost, safety, and next steps.

## Law 4 — Proof before promise

Use verified outcomes, partner confirmations, testimonials with consent, transparent reporting, and specific operational evidence. Do not use guarantees, invented scarcity, inflated counts, or implied endorsements.

## Law 5 — Reduce friction

Every additional field, click, decision, delay, or unclear requirement lowers completion probability. Ask only for information required to complete the action safely and effectively.

## Law 6 — Earn attention immediately

The opening must communicate subject, stakes, or payoff quickly. Do not begin with logos, long introductions, organizational history, or generic greetings unless they are the story.

## Law 7 — Retention requires progression

Each segment should add information, deepen emotion, reveal progress, resolve a question, or open a more important one. Remove repeated points and dead time.

Use ethical retention mechanics:

- clear promise;
- visible progression;
- unanswered but honest questions;
- pattern changes that support comprehension;
- specific detail;
- earned payoff.

Never use misleading thumbnails, false countdowns, bait-and-switch framing, humiliation, or unsafe spectacle.

## Law 8 — Emotion must be earned

Emotion should come from human truth, aspiration, effort, relationship, and consequence. Music, editing, and copy may support emotion but must not manufacture a claim the footage cannot sustain.

## Law 9 — One asset, one movement step

Every asset should move the audience one step:

Awareness → Interest → Trust → Participation → Advocacy

Do not ask a cold audience for the deepest commitment without first supplying context and trust.

## Law 10 — Measure conversion quality

Track the action and its downstream quality:

- registrations that attend;
- volunteer leads that complete onboarding;
- sponsor leads that schedule meetings;
- donations that can be acknowledged and reported;
- content that generates qualified questions;
- repeat participants and referrals.

Reach is diagnostic, not the final outcome.

## Approval checklist

- Is the audience clearly defined?
- Is the desired outcome concrete?
- Is the organization positioned as guide rather than hero?
- Is the primary action obvious?
- Are time, effort, eligibility, and next steps clear?
- Is every promise supported?
- Has friction been removed without weakening safety?
- Does the opening earn attention honestly?
- Does each section progress rather than repeat?
- Is the success metric tied to real participation?
