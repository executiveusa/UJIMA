Three columnsInstagram presents the profile in three vertical columns. We use those three spaces as a repeating weekly rhythm.
Four rowsEach week completes one horizontal row. Four weeks create four rows, or twelve primary Feed posts.
One visual systemMonday establishes identity. Wednesday carries the video story. Friday brings community and event energy.
Independent, not fragileEach post stands on its own. We are not building a giant puzzle that breaks when one post changes.
How the row is built

Instagram places the newest post first

The row is published in one order and displayed in another. We plan for that before anything is scheduled.

Step 01

Monday

Publishes first and becomes the right tile by Friday.

Step 02

Wednesday

The Reel cover publishes second and becomes the center tile.

Step 03

Friday

The community image publishes last and becomes the left tile.

Why ASC3ND is doing this

Consistency builds trust before conversion

People understand the organization fasterThe profile immediately shows mission, stories, programs and community action.
The campaign feels plannedEvery post belongs to a wider story instead of looking improvised.
Video and images support each otherReels bring voice and emotion. Static posts create clarity and visual memory.
The system continues after the eventThe same structure can support future programs, events and impact stories.
Agent protection

No agent posts blindly

Before scheduling through Postiz, the agent checks the current profile, simulates the proposed post, verifies the approved row, and stops if a manual post, pin, deletion or layout change would break the sequence.

Read the current profileInspect the newest posts and pinned items.
Compare with the grid planConfirm the intended row, column and visual family.
Preview before publishingShow how the profile will look after the proposed post.
