# ASC3ND Social Card Polish Rules

## Purpose
Lock the visual restraint for ASC3ND social cards so every agent produces a consistent, classy system instead of noisy, competing-color graphics.

## Governing rule
Use hierarchy, whitespace, typography, and one controlled accent to create energy. Do not use multiple competing colors to manufacture excitement.

## Typography lock
- Display: Barlow Condensed
  - Black 900 for primary headlines
  - ExtraBold 800 for secondary display
  - Bold 700 for labels
- Body: Barlow
  - Regular 400
  - Medium 500
  - SemiBold 600
  - Bold 700
- Maximum font families per asset: 2
- No random serif, decorative script, novelty face, stretched type, or AI pseudo-lettering for core copy.

## Color discipline
### Black / identity cards
- Background: near-black
- Primary type: warm white
- Accent: ASC3ND orange
- Optional secondary accent: teal, but only if it solves a clear hierarchy problem
- Never let orange and teal compete at equal visual weight

### White / community cards
- Background: warm white / cream
- Primary type: near-black
- Accent: ASC3ND orange
- Teal is secondary only
- No additional saturated colors in the main composition

### Documentary / real-human covers
- Use real ASC3ND photography or real video frames only
- White or near-white primary typography
- One accent color maximum per cover
- Never place high-saturation accents over faces

## Steve Krug-style clarity rules
- One dominant message per card
- Viewer should understand the card in 2 seconds
- The primary headline must win immediately
- Event logistics must be scannable without decoding layout tricks
- Remove any visual element that does not improve comprehension, hierarchy, trust, or action
- No color should be the only carrier of meaning

## Composition rules
- Maximum 1 dominant headline
- Maximum 1 supporting paragraph or 3 short support lines
- Use negative space intentionally
- No generic centered card stack
- No repeated boxes unless information structure requires them
- Avoid excessive icons
- Avoid competing borders, gradients, shadows, decorative marks, and multiple underlines
- Brush marks may support one key emphasis, not decorate every section

## Human imagery law
- No AI-generated people
- No synthetic youth
- No fake mentors
- No fake volunteers
- No fake event crowds
- No fake barbershop scenes
- Use real footage, real approved photos, or typography-only treatments

## Event fact law
Use only approved campaign facts for Community Cuts. Never invent dates, times, venues, addresses, eligibility, sponsors, outcomes, or registration requirements.

## QA rejection rules
Reject the asset if:
- more than two saturated accent colors compete
- the headline is not immediately dominant
- the asset looks like a generic Canva template
- type hierarchy requires explanation
- human imagery is synthetic or unverified
- text competes with the image instead of supporting it
- there are unnecessary icons, dividers, borders, or decorative marks
- the card feels busy, harsh, or visually exhausting

## Acceptance test
The asset should feel:
- classy
- simple
- confident
- easy on the eyes
- intentional
- recognizably ASC3ND

The strongest version is usually the one with fewer visual decisions, not more.
