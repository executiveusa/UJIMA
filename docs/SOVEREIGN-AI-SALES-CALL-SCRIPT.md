# Sales Call Script — Asc3nd Mission OS™

**Type:** Internal operator / sales staff reference  
**Status:** Gate 5A — ready for Architect review before client use  
**Use:** Discovery calls and follow-up conversations with prospective nonprofit and social-purpose clients

---

## Before the call

Review the prospect's website. Know:
- What programs they run
- How large they are (staff, budget estimate if visible)
- Whether they mention AI, technology, or grant work on their site
- Whether they are in the Pacific Northwest

Do not promise specific outcomes. Do not quote pricing until after discovery questions are complete.

---

## Call structure (30 minutes)

| Phase | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 2 min | Build rapport, set agenda |
| Discovery | 15 min | Understand workflows, pain, fit |
| Offer overview | 8 min | Explain Mission OS and ownership model |
| Objections | 3 min | Address concerns |
| Close / next step | 2 min | Define follow-up |

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

> "Thanks for taking the time today. I want to use this call to understand your organization's work and your current operations — not to pitch you on something. If Mission OS turns out to be a fit, great. If not, I'll tell you that honestly. Does that work?"

Set the agenda:
> "I'll ask you a few questions about how your team operates, what's working and what isn't, and then I'll give you an overview of what we actually do and what you'd own after we work together. Sound good?"

---

## Discovery questions (15 minutes)

Work through these questions. Take notes. Not every question is appropriate for every call — read the conversation.

### Organization and programs

1. **Tell me about your organization. What programs do you run?**  
   *(Listen for: number of programs, staff size, grant-funded vs. fee-based)*

2. **How many staff do you have? Is there anyone who handles operations or technology specifically?**  
   *(Listen for: operational capacity, whether there is a tech-savvy person)*

3. **What does a typical week look like for your team in terms of administrative work?**  
   *(Listen for: grant writing, reporting, communications, intake, scheduling)*

### Current tools and pain

4. **What tools do you currently use to manage your work — email, project management, CRM, anything?**  
   *(Listen for: existing tech stack, GoHighLevel, Salesforce, Bloomerang, spreadsheets)*

5. **Have you tried any AI tools already — ChatGPT, Copilot, anything like that?**  
   *(Listen for: experience level, positive/negative reactions, concerns about data privacy)*

6. **Where does your team spend the most time on repetitive or drafting work?**  
   *(Listen for: grant applications, donor communications, program reports, social media, intake paperwork)*

7. **Have you had any experiences where a technology tool created more work than it saved, or where you lost control of your data or workflow?**  
   *(Listen for: vendor lock-in, overbuilt systems, data concerns — this surfaces Mission OS's ownership model as the answer)*

### Grant and funding work

8. **How much of your revenue comes from grants? How many applications does your team manage per year?**  
   *(Listen for: grant-dependence, whether agent drafting assistance would have high value)*

9. **Who writes your grants today? Is that a bottleneck?**  
   *(Listen for: executive director doing it all, no dedicated development staff)*

### Data sensitivity

10. **Does your organization work with data that has privacy requirements — health information, youth data, immigration status, anything like that?**  
    *(Listen for: HIPAA, FERPA, COPPA exposure — flag these clearly: "We don't certify for compliance, and we'd want you to talk to legal counsel before deploying any AI system that touches that data.")*

### Fit and readiness

11. **Do you have someone on your team — even part-time — who could learn to administer a system and act as your internal operator?**  
    *(Listen for: willingness to have a "system owner" — this is a requirement for successful deployment)*

12. **What would success look like for you six months after a system like this was running?**  
    *(Listen for: specific outcomes — this helps scope the right tier)*

## Offer overview (8 minutes)

**After discovery, pivot with:**

> "Based on what you've described, let me tell you what Mission OS actually is and whether I think it's a fit."

### The ownership pitch

> "The core thing that makes Mission OS different is that you own it. After we set it up, it runs on your server, your data stays in your database, and you have the source code. There is no ongoing subscription to us. You can hire any other developer to maintain it. You are not renting anything."

### What the agents do

> "Your agents help your staff draft content — grant proposals, emails, social media posts, program summaries. Every output goes to a human for review before it goes anywhere. Agents cannot submit anything, send anything, or publish anything without a person approving it first. That is not an optional setting — it is built in."

### What you get

> "You get the control panel — your ops dashboard — where your team can see every agent action, approve or reject outputs, and track what's being generated. The operator manual tells your system owner how to run everything. And we train your team during a 14-day structured onboarding after we go live."

### What it costs (if ready to discuss)

> "Pricing depends on scope. After a discovery like this one, we scope a proposal. Starter setups — smaller orgs, one or two programs — run in the low thousands for setup. Larger orgs with active grant programs and more complex workflows are higher. I'll give you a scoped proposal after this call if you'd like one. All pricing is in draft — final numbers come after we agree on scope."

> **Do not quote specific numbers verbally unless the pricing sheet has been approved by the Architect. Always say "draft figures" and "final pricing is scoped per engagement."**

## Common objections

### "We already have a website / CRM / tool"

> "Mission OS is not a replacement for your website, your CRM, or your email. It sits alongside those tools. Agents help your staff work faster — they don't replace your existing systems."

### "We're not a tech organization"

> "You don't need to be. The system is designed so one person — your operator — can manage it. We train that person during onboarding. The dashboard is built for non-technical staff."

### "We're worried about AI hallucinating or making mistakes"

> "That is exactly why every agent output goes to a human before it goes anywhere. Agents are drafting tools. Your staff reviews everything. If something looks wrong, they reject it. Nothing goes out without approval."

### "We don't have the budget"

> "That is fair, and I want to be honest with you. Mission OS is not free, and the setup requires investment. What I can offer is a scoped proposal so you can see exactly what you'd get and what it would cost. If it is not the right fit financially right now, that is a legitimate answer."

### "We handle sensitive data — donors, youth, health"

> "I am glad you raised that. We do not certify Mission OS for HIPAA, FERPA, or any other compliance framework. If you have data in those categories, you need to talk to your attorney before deploying any AI system — not just Mission OS, but any of them. We can be part of that conversation, but we cannot make that decision for you."

### "What happens if Asc3nd goes away?"

> "You own the system. You own the source code under MIT license. You own all your data. If Asc3nd disappeared tomorrow, your system would keep running and you could hire any developer to maintain it. That is by design."

## Close / next step (2 minutes)

If fit looks good:
> "What I'd suggest is this: I will put together a scoped proposal based on what we discussed today. It will describe what setup would look like for your organization, what is included, and draft pricing. You review it with your team, and then we decide together whether to move forward. No obligation. Does that work?"

If fit is unclear:
> "I want to think about what we discussed and make sure I am recommending the right thing. Can I follow up with a short written summary and my honest take on whether Mission OS is the right fit for you?"

If not a fit:
> "Based on what you've told me, I don't think Mission OS is the right fit right now. [Explain why honestly.] I don't want to waste your time. If things change — if you add staff, get a grant for technology, or your situation shifts — reach out and we can revisit."
